# Superpowers pack

The skill pack for Superpowers `7.0.2` (the plugin's `skills/` folder). It says which Superpowers skill carries out each phase of the flow and where a ShipIt rule beats a Superpowers default. The rules of ShipIt always win; a skill is followed as written except where an item below says otherwise. A line number is the line of the file named, in that version.

## Phases

- goal and roadmap: `superpowers:brainstorming`. The owner's goal and its numbered done-when items, then the roadmap; the roadmap ends with the decision point on how slices run.
- build plan: `superpowers:writing-plans`. One build plan for each slice, in the `build-plan` form.
- make: `superpowers:subagent-driven-development`, `superpowers:test-driven-development`, `superpowers:systematic-debugging`, `superpowers:using-git-worktrees`. The maker works through the steps of the build plan in a slice worktree, test first.
- check: `superpowers:verification-before-completion`. The command is `shipit check`.
- review: `superpowers:requesting-code-review` (its checklist only) and `superpowers:dispatching-parallel-agents` (the reader first, then the informed review and the second opinion in parallel).
- fix round: `superpowers:receiving-code-review`, `superpowers:systematic-debugging`, `superpowers:test-driven-development`. Each finding is verified before it is fixed.
- ship: `superpowers:finishing-a-development-branch`, only its "verify first" step and opening the pull request.
- `superpowers:executing-plans`: only when the owner chose inline execution at the roadmap's decision point, or no subagent tool exists.
- `superpowers:using-superpowers`: how skills are found; see the override on skill priority.
- `superpowers:writing-skills`: only when a skill of ShipIt is written or changed.
- `superpowers:diagnosing-superpowers`: only when the owner asks why a session went wrong.

## Overrides

- `superpowers:subagent-driven-development` (SKILL.md:255-259) · a `task` review after every `task`, with spec and quality both required · no review after each step of a build plan; after the last step and a passing `shipit check`, one informed review and one second opinion run, and the reader runs first · the maker works through the whole build plan; the lead starts the reader and the two reviews at the end.
- `superpowers:subagent-driven-development` (SKILL.md:140-175) · the controller picks the cheapest model that fits each role, reviewers a mid tier or above · tiers and effort come from the setup: the maker's role, the reader's role and a reviewer one tier above whoever made the work (`agents.roles`, `agents.tiers`) · name the tier's model and its effort in every dispatch; never inherit the session's and never choose by price.
- `superpowers:subagent-driven-development` (references/fix-loop.md:21-34, 59-77) · five fix rounds for each `task`, rounds 4 and 5 on a stronger model, then the controller rules on what is left (the breaker) · at most `agents.max_fix_rounds` rounds for the slice, each ending in a check and a new review; no breaker, no ruling by the lead · when the rounds are used up, the open findings are written down as known issues with a reason and go to the owner.
- `superpowers:subagent-driven-development` (SKILL.md:19-31) · "rulings, not stalls": the controller decides conflicts and gaps and logs a `Ruling` line, and only four kinds of thing stop it · a change to the goal, the roadmap, a shared contract or a done-when item is the owner's to decide, never a ruling · stop and ask with the `decision-point` form (`shipit template decision-point`); every other decision goes in the logbook.
- `superpowers:subagent-driven-development` (SKILL.md:27-31) and `superpowers:finishing-a-development-branch` (SKILL.md:53-126) · a merge or a push is a soft stop that asks first, and the finish step offers merge locally, push and pull request, keep, or discard · only the owner ships (rule 4): the finish step opens the pull request and stops; no merge, no tag, no push to the base branch, no menu · run `shipit verify`, open the pull request from the slice branch, say so, and hand it to the owner.
- `superpowers:writing-plans` (SKILL.md:16), `superpowers:brainstorming` (SKILL.md:246) and `superpowers:subagent-driven-development` (SKILL.md:113-118) · the build plan goes to `docs/superpowers/plans/`, the design to `docs/superpowers/specs/`, and a ledger with briefs to `.superpowers/sdd/` · the flow keeps its files in the store, not in the project: goal, roadmap, build plan and logbook under `<git common dir>/shipit/goals/<goal>/` · the build plan is `goals/<goal>/slices/<slice>/build-plan.md` and the logbook replaces the ledger.
- `superpowers:writing-plans` (SKILL.md:52-90, 163-177) · a fixed header with a tech stack and a spec line, and a self-review the writer runs alone · the build plan is the `build-plan` form (`shipit template build-plan`) with its done-when coverage block, checked by `shipit validate build-plan` and then reviewed by a reviewer one tier above the planner · fill in the form's parts and validate the file; run the self-review list before that review, not in place of it.
- `superpowers:writing-plans` (SKILL.md:179-204) · after writing, ask the human to pick subagent-driven or native execution for the `plan` · how slices run is one decision point, asked once at the end of `shipit-roadmap`, with the lead's recommendation · do not ask again for each build plan; hand it over with the answer already known.
- `superpowers:executing-plans` (SKILL.md:8-10, 85-86, 255-259) · inline execution, a self-review by the implementer and one whole-branch review at the end · the maker never confirms its own work (rule 1), and the reader explains the diff first · after the last step run `shipit check`, then the reader, then the informed review and the second opinion.
- `superpowers:requesting-code-review` (SKILL.md:32-46) · a reviewer returns strengths, issues graded Critical, Important and Minor, and an assessment, over a diff file · a review is the `informed-review` or `second-opinion` form: findings written as Conventional Comments that the owner reads as must fix, nice to fix and must answer, a result of `approve`, `approve-after-fixes` or `rework`, and Security and Performance answered every time, with no praise · fill the form from `shipit template <kind>`, check it with `shipit validate`, and record it as the receipt.
- `superpowers:verification-before-completion` (SKILL.md:14-48) · fresh command output before any claim of success · the evidence for a slice is the receipt of `shipit check`; nothing counts without a receipt (rule 2) · run `shipit check`, then `shipit verify`; a claim of done without a valid receipt is refused.
- `superpowers:brainstorming` (SKILL.md:182-187, 277) · a spike the human agreed to gets no tests and no review process · a spike is a named throwaway that settles one risk of the roadmap, is never shipped, and leaves a note in the logbook · keeping its code is a new slice with its own build plan.
- `superpowers:brainstorming` (SKILL.md:52-80, 156) · questions to the human are free-form, asked one at a time in the session's own words · every question the owner must answer is a `decision-point` form with What, Context, Recommendation and Next, within `owner.max_lines` lines (a fenced block whose info string is `example` does not count there; its lines, fence lines included, count toward `owner.example_max_lines`) · write it from `shipit template decision-point` and check it with `shipit validate decision-point`.
- `superpowers:using-git-worktrees` (SKILL.md:63-96) · the worktree goes in `.worktrees/` at the project root, and an ignore rule is added and committed when it is missing · a slice commits only the files it owns, so an ignore change is a slice of its own · the lead makes the worktree outside the project, `../wt/<slice>`, on the branch `slice/<goal>/<slice>`.
- `superpowers:subagent-driven-development` (SKILL.md:235) · never run several implementers in parallel, because they conflict · slices run in parallel, each in its own worktree, when the roadmap says they may (their `owns` do not overlap); inside one slice there is one maker · the caps are `agents.parallel.slices` for slices and `agents.parallel.agents` for agents at once.
- `superpowers:using-superpowers` (SKILL.md:26-31, 63-65) · process skills come first, and the skill check comes before any answer · the ShipIt skill of the phase comes first (`shipit`, `shipit-roadmap`, `shipit-slice`, `shipit-test-drive`), then the Superpowers skills named in Phases carry it out · when a rule of ShipIt and a Superpowers default disagree, follow the rule.
- `superpowers:dispatching-parallel-agents` (SKILL.md:12-14) · one agent for each independent problem, all started together · the reader runs first and alone, on the diff without the goal; then the informed review (given the reader's explanation) and the second opinion (given nothing but the work) run in parallel · start the three in that order and pass the explanation only to the informed review.
- `superpowers:systematic-debugging` (SKILL.md:210) · after failed fixes, stop and discuss with the human partner before trying more · the number of fix rounds is `agents.max_fix_rounds`, and a question to the owner is a `decision-point` form, never an open talk · when the rounds are used up, write the open finding down as a known issue with a reason (rule 3) and hand it to the owner; ask only with the form, and only when the goal or a shared contract must change.
- `superpowers:test-driven-development` (SKILL.md:24, 312, 330) · an exception to test first needs the human partner's permission, and a test that cannot be thought of is asked about · every owner question goes through the `decision-point` form, and the build plan already names the test for each step · do not ask; if a step has no test, write that in the logbook as a gap in the build plan and take it to the planner, or to the owner with the form.
